# TMS v2 — V1 Design

A number-free, append-only epistemic ledger. Humans and AI agents supply interpretations; the ledger preserves assertions, relationships, reasons, and provenance.

[db/init.sql](db/init.sql) defines the storage schema and [tms/server.py](tms/server.py) implements the MCP surface below. Contributor roles live in [ROLES.md](ROLES.md), with worked records in [EXAMPLES.md](EXAMPLES.md).

[db/verify.sql](db/verify.sql) is a `psql` schema check to run after `db/init.sql`; it exercises storage invariants and an append/research/correction scenario, not an application server.

## 1. Boundary

The ledger does not determine truth, evidence applicability, or belief. Conclusions such as **believed**, **contested**, and **open or resolved contradiction** belong entirely to downstream human or AI readers. They are neither stored statuses nor server-derived results.

There are no confidence scales, confidence aggregation, automatic propagation, or rules for deactivating records. Numbers in opaque claim text remain ordinary content. Retrieval distances are retrieval aids, not confidence.

Claims about identity, classification, or source reliability are ordinary statements. The ledger does not interpret them, merge identities, redirect references, or treat distinct source IDs as proof of independent reporting.

## 2. Stored Records

The schema is PostgreSQL + pgvector. See [db/init.sql](db/init.sql) for columns and constraints.

| Record | Purpose |
|---|---|
| `authors` | Operational identity registry: ID, name, kind (`human` or `agent`), and assertion time. Not an authentication system or verified-identity guarantee. |
| `collections` | A topic or project. Observations, claims and contradictions belong to exactly one collection; sources and authors are global. The same document imported into two collections is two observations. |
| `sources` | Where material came from, separate from the author who registered it. |
| `observations` | Immutable document records linked to sources. Preserve content for audit; nullable content must be presented as unavailable, not reconstructed from a hash or assumed accessible at a URI. An observation without content cannot ground evidence. |
| `claims` | Opaque text statements, including any relevant event timeframe. |
| `contradictions` | Authored findings explaining why a specified set of claims is incompatible. |
| `contradiction_claims` | A finding's immutable membership: at least two distinct claim IDs. |
| `evidence` | A grounding observation's bearing on one typed target, with a verbatim `quote` from that observation and a specific, checkable reason. A trigger rejects quotes that do not occur in the observation's stored content (forgiving case, whitespace, curly quotes, dashes and ligatures). |

Each of the six content types—collection, source, observation, claim, contradiction, and evidence—requires an `author_id` referencing the registry and a database-supplied `asserted_at`. No run or session metadata is needed.

A contradiction is an explicit contributor assertion, not something inferred automatically from claim text or evidence. Its membership is complete when created and cannot be extended or reduced afterward. A different membership requires a new finding.

### Evidence vocabulary

Every evidence record identifies a grounding observation, exactly one target, a relation, a verbatim quote of at most 1500 characters from the grounding observation, and a nonblank reason:

- `SUPPORTS`: grounds for the target.
- `REBUTS`: grounds against the target.
- `QUALIFIES`: a condition or caveat concerning a **claim only**.

Targets may be a **claim, evidence record, source, observation, or contradiction**. Grounding an evidence record in an observation is different from targeting that observation. Evidence targeting a contradiction can support or rebut the finding; it does not close, erase, or change it. Likewise, evidence about a source or another evidence record does not automatically affect linked claims. Reviewers append further grounded evidence; readers interpret the whole record.

## 3. History and Retrieval

All records are append-only, without soft deletion. Corrections add observations, claims, contradiction findings, or evidence as appropriate; earlier records remain available. There is no implicit replacement, retirement, or merge. Repeated content hashes and identical claim text are valid distinct records, not uniqueness or idempotency keys.

`asserted_at` records entry into the ledger, not when a statement was true. Event time stays in text. An `as_of` read restricts records to those asserted at or before the cutoff; it does not compute a historical belief state. `after` supports discovery of later assertions.

Claim text may be embedded in the index layer (`index_entries`), not on the ledger tables. Each embedding row records the model that produced it and its dimensions. One event can carry rows from more than one model; vector search compares a single model at a time and never mixes them. The initial model is **`Qwen/Qwen3-Embedding-8B`**, retaining all **4096 dimensions**. Use exact cosine retrieval, not an incompatible HNSW index. PostgreSQL keyword/text retrieval provides another selectable method; callers must be able to retrieve records without vectors. Retrieval results identify the method used. Neither a match nor its absence establishes relevance, duplication, contradiction, truth, or completeness.

Memberships, evidence links, authors, and timestamps allow readers to browse an evidence trail. They do not establish why research was commissioned or provide a causal trace of a research process. There is no separate assessment log or research-motivation field.

## 4. MCP Surface

Tools perform storage and retrieval, not contributors' reasoning. Exact reads return raw record objects. Expose author IDs and assertion times; include evidence quotes, reasons and grounding/target references, and make full observations and sources available through exact reads. Search results must not masquerade as complete document content. Every write and every collection-scoped read takes a `collection`; `TMS_COLLECTION` supplies a default. The server runs over stdio for a local chat agent and over streamable HTTP behind a bearer token for remote agents.

### Append tools

| Tool | Obligation |
|---|---|
| `add_author` | Register a human or agent identity. |
| `add_collection` | Create a collection. |
| `add_source` | Record a source and its registering author. |
| `add_observation` | Record an observation, its source, content, and author into a collection. Given a URI and no content, the server fetches and extracts the text; the stored text is what quotes are checked against. |
| `add_claim` | Record the opaque statement and author; the server embeds it for vector retrieval. |
| `add_contradiction` | Atomically write a finding, its reason, author, and all of its at least two distinct claim members. |
| `add_evidence` | Record author, grounding observation, typed target, relation, verbatim quote, and specific reason; enforce claims-only `QUALIFIES` and reject quotes absent from the observation. |

The database supplies assertion timestamps and IDs are server-issued with a kind prefix (`src_`, `o_`, `c_`, `k_`, `e_`). Writes do not promise content-based deduplication. There are no update, delete, evaluation, or resolution tools.

### Read tools

| Tool | Purpose |
|---|---|
| `get_record` | Exact lookup by kind and ID; contradiction reads include member claim IDs; observation reads page through content. |
| `get_evidence` | Filter by paired `target_kind`/`target_id`, `from_observation_id`, and/or `relation`. |
| `search_claims` | Vector or keyword retrieval within a collection; each result inlines its evidence and contradiction IDs. |
| `list_observations` | Observations in a collection, optionally only those no evidence cites yet. |
| `search_sources` | Candidate lookup by name or URI, without automatic identity matching. |
| `search_contradictions` | Findings in a collection by member claim ID and/or reason text, with members and evidence targeting the finding. |
| `list_collections` | Collections with counts. |
| `check_proof` | Validate a thinking proof against the ledger; see below. |

There are no status filters or belief summaries. Pagination, `as_of` and `after` cutoffs, and observation vector search are deferred until a consumer needs them.

Tool descriptions guide **search → inspect → append → read**: inspect candidate records and underlying sources, reuse IDs deliberately, and append only with the required provenance, quotes and reasons. These are contributor obligations, not an intake pipeline or hidden server interpretation. Two MCP prompts, `briefing` and `claims_pass`, state the obligations for the two common workflows.

## 5. Thinking Proofs

A thinking proof is the answering agent's argument for an answer, kept outside the ledger. It is a DAG whose leaves are ledger record IDs and whose internal nodes are the agent's inference steps: `{"question", "steps": [{"id", "say", "from": [...], "gap"?}]}`. `check_proof` enforces that every `from` entry is a record in the collection or an earlier step, that exactly one step is the root, and that every step has a ledger record in its ancestry unless marked `gap`. It returns the problems and an indented tree with each leaf expanded to its text, quote, source and author. The inference steps themselves are the agent's interpretation and are not validated. The agent saves proofs as files beside its answers.

## 6. Scope

V1 is the schema, the MCP server, the two prompts and the proof check. It is not an evaluator, workflow engine, or autonomous researcher. The role guidance and examples explain how humans and agents can use this ledger without making their conclusions database rules. [RESEARCH_LOOP.md](RESEARCH_LOOP.md) preserves historical discussion rather than adding requirements to this design.
