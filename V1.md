# TMS v2 — V1 Design

A number-free, append-only epistemic ledger. Humans and AI agents supply interpretations; the ledger preserves assertions, relationships, reasons, and provenance.

[db/init.sql](db/init.sql) defines the implemented storage schema. The MCP surface below is a proposal, not an implemented server. Contributor roles live in [ROLES.md](ROLES.md), with worked records in [EXAMPLES.md](EXAMPLES.md).

[db/verify.sql](db/verify.sql) is a `psql` schema check to run after `db/init.sql`; it exercises storage invariants and an append/research/correction scenario, not an application server.

## 1. Boundary

The ledger does not determine truth, evidence applicability, or belief. Conclusions such as **believed**, **contested**, and **open or resolved contradiction** belong entirely to downstream human or AI readers. They are neither stored statuses nor server-derived results.

There are no confidence scales, confidence aggregation, automatic propagation, or rules for deactivating records. Numbers in opaque claim text remain ordinary content. Retrieval distances are retrieval aids, not confidence.

Claims about identity, classification, or source reliability are ordinary statements. The ledger does not interpret them, merge identities, redirect references, or treat distinct source IDs as proof of independent reporting.

## 2. Stored Records

The schema is PostgreSQL + pgvector. See [db/init.sql](db/init.sql) for columns and constraints.

| Record | Purpose |
|---|---|
| `authors` | Operational identity registry: ID, name, kind (`human` or `agent`), and assertion time. Not an authentication system or verified-identity guarantee. |
| `sources` | Where material came from, separate from the author who registered it. |
| `observations` | Immutable document records linked to sources. Preserve content for audit; nullable content must be presented as unavailable, not reconstructed from a hash or assumed accessible at a URI. |
| `claims` | Opaque text statements, including any relevant event timeframe. |
| `contradictions` | Authored findings explaining why a specified set of claims is incompatible. |
| `contradiction_claims` | A finding's immutable membership: at least two distinct claim IDs. |
| `evidence` | A grounding observation's bearing on one typed target, with a specific, checkable reason. |

Each of the five content types—source, observation, claim, contradiction, and evidence—requires an `author_id` referencing the registry and a database-supplied `asserted_at`. No run or session metadata is needed.

A contradiction is an explicit contributor assertion, not something inferred automatically from claim text or evidence. Its membership is complete when created and cannot be extended or reduced afterward. A different membership requires a new finding.

### Evidence vocabulary

Every evidence record identifies a grounding observation, exactly one target, a relation, and a nonblank reason:

- `SUPPORTS`: grounds for the target.
- `REBUTS`: grounds against the target.
- `QUALIFIES`: a condition or caveat concerning a **claim only**.

Targets may be a **claim, evidence record, source, observation, or contradiction**. Grounding an evidence record in an observation is different from targeting that observation. Evidence targeting a contradiction can support or rebut the finding; it does not close, erase, or change it. Likewise, evidence about a source or another evidence record does not automatically affect linked claims. Reviewers append further grounded evidence; readers interpret the whole record.

## 3. History and Retrieval

All records are append-only, without soft deletion. Corrections add observations, claims, contradiction findings, or evidence as appropriate; earlier records remain available. There is no implicit replacement, retirement, or merge. Repeated content hashes and identical claim text are valid distinct records, not uniqueness or idempotency keys.

`asserted_at` records entry into the ledger, not when a statement was true. Event time stays in text. An `as_of` read restricts records to those asserted at or before the cutoff; it does not compute a historical belief state. `after` supports discovery of later assertions.

Claims and observations may have embeddings; vector and model are either both present or both absent. The initial model is **`Qwen/Qwen3-Embedding-8B`**, retaining all **4096 dimensions**. Use exact cosine retrieval, not an incompatible HNSW index. PostgreSQL keyword/text retrieval provides another selectable method; callers must be able to retrieve records without vectors. Retrieval results identify the method used. Neither a match nor its absence establishes relevance, duplication, contradiction, truth, or completeness.

Memberships, evidence links, authors, and timestamps allow readers to browse an evidence trail. They do not establish why research was commissioned or provide a causal trace of a research process. There is no separate assessment log or research-motivation field.

## 4. Proposed MCP Surface

Tools perform storage and retrieval, not contributors' reasoning. Exact reads return raw record objects. Collection queries return `items`, `page`, `page_size`, and `total_count`, with deterministic ordering and an ID tie-breaker. Expose author IDs and assertion times; include evidence reasons and grounding/target references, and make full observations and sources available through exact reads. Search excerpts must not masquerade as complete document content.

### Append tools

| Tool | Obligation |
|---|---|
| `add_author` | Register a human or agent identity. |
| `add_source` | Record a source and its registering author. |
| `add_observation` | Record an observation, its source, available content, and author. |
| `add_claim` | Record the opaque statement and author. |
| `add_contradiction` | Atomically write a finding, its reason, author, and all of its at least two distinct claim members. |
| `add_evidence` | Record author, grounding observation, typed target, relation, and specific reason; enforce claims-only `QUALIFIES`. |

The database supplies assertion timestamps. Writes do not promise content-based deduplication. There are no update, delete, evaluation, or resolution tools.

### Read tools

| Tool | Purpose |
|---|---|
| `get_author`, `get_source`, `get_observation`, `get_claim`, `get_contradiction` | Exact ID lookup; contradiction reads include complete member claim IDs and the finding's reason. |
| `get_evidence` | Query by optional `evidence_id`, paired `target_kind`/`target_id`, `from_observation_id`, and/or `relation`; returns a paginated collection, with at most one item for an exact evidence ID. |
| `search_claims` | Search by text or an example claim (`similar_to`), with selectable `vector` or `keyword` text retrieval; no text or example lists records. |
| `search_observations` | Search by text or an example observation (`similar_to`), with selectable `vector` or `keyword` text retrieval, plus source ID and exact URI/content-hash filters; no text or example lists records. |
| `search_sources` | Candidate lookup by name or URI, without automatic identity matching. |
| `search_contradictions` | Find findings by member claim ID and/or reason text. |

Collection queries accept `page`/`page_size` and, where applicable, `author_id`, `after`, and `as_of`. Exact reads may use `as_of` to respect a ledger cutoff. There are no status filters or belief summaries. Downstream consumers join retrieved records to browse related evidence; no trace or diff tool is required.

Tool descriptions should guide **search → inspect → append → read**: inspect candidate records and underlying sources, reuse IDs deliberately, and append only with the required provenance and reasons. These are contributor obligations, not an intake pipeline or hidden server interpretation.

## 5. Scope

V1 is the schema plus the proposed mechanical MCP interface above. It is not an evaluator, workflow engine, or autonomous researcher. The role guidance and examples explain how humans and agents can use this ledger without making their conclusions database rules. [RESEARCH_LOOP.md](RESEARCH_LOOP.md) preserves historical discussion rather than adding requirements to this design.
